LLDP-Runner Roadmap

This file tracks planned work, unresolved design questions, production-derived fixtures, and ideas that may become future features. It is a working outline, not a release promise. Items can move, split, or be removed when live network evidence shows that another design is safer.

Released behavior is documented in the source, operator documentation, and CHANGELOG.md. An unchecked roadmap item is not implemented unless a later release entry says otherwise.

Each unfinished item includes three planning notes: Remaining work describes the implementation boundary, Operational benefit explains why an operator might prioritize it, and Estimated effort is a relative engineering estimate that includes implementation, tests, documentation, and production validation.

Guiding principles

  • Preserve what each device reported before applying identity or topology reasoning.
  • Treat CDP, LLDP, LLDPv2, IF-MIB, ENTITY-MIB, DNS, and seed input as typed evidence with provenance and scope.
  • Prefer an explicit ambiguous or conflicted result over a confident false merge.
  • Make repeated processing idempotent: replaying the same evidence produces the same graph.
  • Keep reasoning reversible: stronger or conflicting evidence can supersede an earlier decision without deleting history.
  • Separate collection coverage from topology state. A failed query means “not observed,” not necessarily “removed.”
  • Keep physical chassis, logical systems, SNMP agents, interfaces, protocol advertisements, and relationships as separate concepts.
  • Preserve deterministic ordering for output, tests, and cross-run comparison.
  • Explain advanced resolver behavior in terms useful to Network Operations staff while retaining enough precision for engineering and architecture review.

Current graph-integrity work

Immutable collection boundary

    • Remaining work: Extend the collection-state schema and each CDP/LLDP/LLDPv2 collector so the unassembled native row, including every index component, is committed before normalization or filtering.
    • Operational benefit: Investigators could reconstruct exactly what the agent returned and distinguish device data defects from parser or row-assembly defects.
    • Estimated effort: Large - requires schema work, collector changes, replay tooling, storage-size review, and production fixtures.
    • Remaining work: Define one table-attempt provenance record and populate it on success, empty results, retries, and terminal failures across every collector path.
    • Operational benefit: Operators could explain why one table succeeded or failed on a particular route without correlating several log streams manually.
    • Estimated effort: Medium to large - cross-cuts session routing, collectors, SQLite persistence, schemas, and diagnostics.
    • Remaining work: Specify coverage states and provenance fields for each future vendor-specific MIB family, then connect them to uncertainty and diff reporting.
    • Operational benefit: New collectors would not silently weaken topology conclusions when a proprietary table is absent, partial, or inaccessible.
    • Estimated effort: Ongoing medium effort per collector family after the shared coverage contract is established.

Graph authority and materialization

    • observation completeness;
    • observation projection status;
    • entity-resolution maturity;
    • physical-topology inference status;
    • directness status; and
    • compatibility-projection role.
    • Remaining work: Model SNMP engine, address, credential route, and context as a first-class graph entity and route all collected evidence through that entity before system resolution.
    • Operational benefit: This would separate the device that answered a query from the logical or physical systems inferred from its data, especially for VDCs and shared management planes.
    • Estimated effort: Large - foundational graph/schema work with broad resolver, projection, diff, and migration impact.
    • Remaining work: Track decision dependencies and component membership so replacing one claim invalidates and rebuilds only graph objects reachable from that decision.
    • Operational benefit: Large runs would resolve faster and operator assertions could be applied without rebuilding the complete graph.
    • Estimated effort: Large - requires dependency indexing, deterministic incremental algorithms, rollback tests, and performance measurement.
    • Remaining work: Replace the legacy compatibility resolver as a parallel authority with a deterministic graph-to-host projection that preserves current output contracts where intended.
    • Operational benefit: One interpretation would drive discovery.json, CSV, and graph views, reducing cross-view disagreement and duplicate bug fixes.
    • Estimated effort: Very large - high-risk architectural convergence requiring extensive production replay and deliberate compatibility decisions.
    • Remaining work: Design configuration/input syntax, validity periods, provenance, conflict handling, and explain output for assertions such as known chassis membership or non-direct links.
    • Operational benefit: Network Operations could encode verified site knowledge while preserving device evidence and seeing where an assertion changes resolution.
    • Estimated effort: Large - needs a claim contract, validation, persistence, graph integration, CLI tooling, and safety documentation.

Stable identifiers and deterministic replay

    • Remaining work: Add repeatable shuffled-replay test passes over representative fixtures and normalize all ordering that still depends on insertion sequence.
    • Operational benefit: This would catch nondeterministic graph IDs or relationships before release and make repeated runs easier to compare.
    • Estimated effort: Medium - mostly test/replay infrastructure, followed by potentially substantial fixes when instability is found.
    • Remaining work: Store key-derivation inputs and expose an explanation comparing old and new claim sets whenever a semantic key changes unexpectedly.
    • Operational benefit: Operators and developers could trace identity churn to one concrete piece of evidence instead of reverse-engineering hashes.
    • Estimated effort: Medium to large - requires stable explanation records, diff presentation, and retention-size controls.

Graph-aware diff

    • Remaining work: Create a compact terminal/JSON presentation that groups the A and B entities, correspondence reasons, conflicts, and affected links for one component.
    • Operational benefit: Complex split/merge results would become reviewable without reading raw graph-diff structures.
    • Estimated effort: Medium - primarily renderer and CLI work after the current correspondence data is normalized for presentation.
    • Remaining work: Define categorical values and derivation rules for each dimension, persist them in diff output, and explain supporting and limiting evidence.
    • Operational benefit: Operators could distinguish a strong identity match with weak collection coverage from a genuinely ambiguous match.
    • Estimated effort: Large - requires an engineering confidence model, fixtures, schema changes, and calibrated operator wording.
    • Remaining work: Build and label a representative truth set, choose measurable outcomes, validate calibration, and define how probabilities coexist with categorical safety states.
    • Operational benefit: A numeric score would be meaningful and testable rather than an arbitrary confidence-looking number.
    • Estimated effort: Very large and data-dependent - blocked on sanitized labeled fixtures and sustained validation work.

Sanitized topology regression fixtures

These sanitized examples are regression-fixture input and design constraints. They are not hard-coded naming or addressing rules.

Nexus 7700 VDC management identities

  • hq-vdc2-7nx1 uses 203.0.113.21 on SVI interface VLAN 61.
  • hq-vdc2-7nx1 uses 203.0.113.24 on mgmt0.
  • hq-vdc3-7nx1 uses 203.0.113.22 on mgmt0.
  • hq-vdc1-7nx1 uses 203.0.113.23 on mgmt0 for that VDC instance.
  • The VDCs are separate logical systems on one physical Nexus 7700 chassis.
  • One VDC can legitimately have more than one management route or interface. Same logical name plus the same verified physical serial can associate those routes with one VDC without merging sibling VDCs.
  • A Catalyst port connected to shared Nexus mgmt0 can report several VDC advertisements on one physical management attachment.
  • The operator-facing default should show one physical chassis with indented logical VDC members.
  • Site naming conventions such as hq-vdc<n>-7nx1 are useful display hints but are never universal identity rules.

hq-vdc1-7nx1 duplicate compatibility projection

The sanitized regression fixture contains two compatibility records with:

  • query target 203.0.113.23;
  • SNMP sysName hq-vdc1-7nx1;
  • physical serial SERIAL000001;
  • the exact same sysUpTime sample; and
  • the same 28 learned-neighbor rows.

The address is confirmed as mgmt0 for the hq-vdc1-7nx1 VDC. The graph represents one logical VDC and retains both source projections as evidence. Version 0.32.3 also prevents an inactive equivalent row from being claimed, defers a READY duplicate while its equivalent projection is active, reconciles remaining duplicates before final output, and folds duplicate complete rows in the compatibility diff. The graph remains the immutable evidence boundary; the compatibility scheduler is not yet graph-derived.

Useful future diagnostics still include:

  • SNMP engine ID and context name for each attempted route;
  • seed, DNS, alias, and observed-neighbor paths that selected the same target;
  • a collection-session ID showing whether both projections consumed one SNMP response; and
  • a resolver explanation that identifies why the duplicate was absorbed.

Transparent discovery-protocol forwarding

A sanitized fixture records a former pass-through-CDP condition at hq-edge-router-01. The intervening switch was configured to block CDP, so downstream Nexus advertisements no longer appear on the router port.

The general case remains valid: an unmanaged switch, transparent bridge, or misconfigured device can forward CDP or LLDP frames. LLDP-Runner therefore retains multi-neighbor observations and does not infer direct versus downstream solely from protocol type. A future directness classifier requires independent corroboration and an operator-visible explanation.

Virtual endpoint movement

00:00:00:00:01:03 appears on more than one switch port and is likely associated with a virtual appliance on VMware ESXi. Such a low-entropy token may identify one logical workload while that workload shifts between hypervisor uplinks. It is insufficient by itself to prove a physical endpoint move.

Future modeling should distinguish:

  • logical workload movement;
  • hypervisor uplink selection;
  • a physical device move; and
  • repeated placeholder or implementation-local identifiers.

End-user workstation appearance

workstation-01 is a generic end-user endpoint that remained connected to branch-floor2-3560-1 across a reboot. The fixture should be reported as an endpoint appearance or renewed observation, not as resolver churn.

Intermittent ENTITY-MIB collection

hq-dc-leaf-02 represents a Nexus-class switch with intermittent ENTITY-MIB results. Missing rows may arise from device software, network conditions, rate limiting, or other external factors. A missing member row is not a confirmed hardware removal unless ENTITY-MIB coverage is complete.

Future graph inspection command

A graph-focused operator command is planned after the graph projection and stable IDs complete another production-validation cycle. The preferred command family is:

lldp-runner graph show
lldp-runner graph explain
lldp-runner graph audit
lldp-runner graph export
lldp-runner graph diff

An optional scripts/lrun-graph launcher may invoke the same subcommands. It must not implement a second diff engine. graph diff should call the same graph correspondence library used by lldp-runner diff.

Planned behavior:

  • show: render entities, interfaces, memberships, and relationship groups;
  • explain: list supporting, conflicting, ignored, and superseded claims for a selected result;
  • audit: report unaccounted evidence, conflicts, contested claims, duplicate projections, and unstable identifiers;
  • export: emit normalized graph JSON or documented CSV views; and
  • diff: provide a graph-focused presentation of the existing many-to-many comparison.

Performance counters

    • Remaining work: Complete phase boundaries for all listed stages, define whether phases overlap, and persist consistent monotonic measurements on every terminal path.
    • Operational benefit: Operators could identify whether a slow run is caused by network collection, graph work, or output/storage finalization.
    • Estimated effort: Medium - some timing exists already, but boundaries, naming, failure paths, tests, and documentation remain incomplete.
    • Remaining work: Instrument worker/session/collector boundaries and aggregate detailed measurements without making high-volume runs excessively large or slow.
    • Operational benefit: Troubleshooting could distinguish slow devices, congested queues, retry-heavy tables, and inefficient transport profiles.
    • Estimated effort: Large - broad instrumentation, storage design, overhead testing, and operator aggregation are required.
    • Remaining work: Choose a bounded set of stable live counters, add them to the scoreboard contract, and design responsive full/compact renderings.
    • Operational benefit: Operators could see the current bottleneck and retry/coverage trend without opening logs during a run.
    • Estimated effort: Medium - depends on performance-counter definitions and requires scoreboard schema plus Watch layout work.
    • Remaining work: Define a stable completion summary covering phase durations, retry/coverage highlights, and the slowest actionable causes without duplicating full reports.
    • Operational benefit: The end of every run would immediately show where time was spent and whether performance warrants investigation.
    • Estimated effort: Small to medium after aggregate counters exist; mainly summary selection, rendering, and tests.
    • Remaining work: Select versioned run-level metrics, populate them for all completed states, and update diff/reporting consumers and schema documentation.
    • Operational benefit: Historical performance and health trends could be computed cheaply from small manifests.
    • Estimated effort: Medium - run-manifest schema change, aggregation, compatibility policy, and regression tests.
    • Remaining work: Aggregate per-target measurements, define deterministic ranking/tie rules, identify dominant causes, and cap output to a useful top set.
    • Operational benefit: Network Operations could prioritize devices or paths that have the greatest effect on run duration.
    • Estimated effort: Medium after detailed timing exists; ranking accuracy and cause attribution need production validation.

Run timeboxes and alerts

    • Remaining work: Add validated configuration, monotonic threshold evaluation, one-shot/repeat policy, and a non-disruptive alert event while workers continue.
    • Operational benefit: Operators could react to unexpectedly long runs before the maintenance window is consumed.
    • Estimated effort: Medium - coordinator policy, logging/manifest events, CLI/config docs, and deterministic time-based tests.
    • Remaining work: Define terminal/resumable status semantics and stop dispatching new targets at the limit while allowing active claims to settle and checkpoint.
    • Operational benefit: A run could respect a hard operational window without abruptly killing SNMP work or corrupting resume state.
    • Estimated effort: Large - touches scheduling, pause/resume, status reporting, finalization, and failure/restart testing.
    • Remaining work: Load comparable historical manifests, select matching run classes, compute a robust baseline, and apply a configurable deviation policy.
    • Operational benefit: Alerts would adapt to the site's normal scale instead of requiring one fixed duration for every discovery mode.
    • Estimated effort: Large - depends on aggregate run metrics and needs history selection, statistical safeguards, and operator explanation.
    • Remaining work: Add a configurable sample floor and explicit insufficient-history status to the baseline evaluator.
    • Operational benefit: New installations or changed run profiles would not trigger misleading alerts from one or two atypical runs.
    • Estimated effort: Small once historical thresholding exists; configuration and edge-case tests are straightforward.
    • Remaining work: Choose and document the statistic, exclude non-comparable terminal states, and test behavior with outliers and sparse history.
    • Operational benefit: The automatic threshold would represent normal operations more reliably when past runs include incidents.
    • Estimated effort: Small to medium within the historical-alert feature; requires careful comparability rules more than code volume.
    • Remaining work: Design a transport-neutral alert event, implement opt-in adapters, validate delivery configuration, and avoid exposing secrets in logs or manifests.
    • Operational benefit: Sites could route run warnings into existing operational notification systems rather than watching a terminal.
    • Estimated effort: Medium to large - journald/syslog are moderate; reliable email configuration, security, and testing add effort.
    • Remaining work: Persist threshold type, configured value, measured value, baseline inputs, action, and timestamp for both triggered and suppressed decisions.
    • Operational benefit: Post-run review could explain exactly why an alert or automatic pause did or did not occur.
    • Estimated effort: Small to medium after threshold features exist; requires a versioned manifest representation and redaction review.

Collection and transport

    • Remaining work: Define hint persistence, freshness/decay, address and credential scoping, operator reset controls, and strict configuration precedence.
    • Operational benefit: Known slow or PDU-sensitive devices could start closer to a reliable profile and reduce avoidable first-attempt failures.
    • Estimated effort: Large - needs durable history, safe invalidation, routing identity, policy controls, and extensive production testing.
    • Remaining work: Collect reproducible fixtures for these signals, define bounded profile changes, and verify that a complete table restart remains deterministic.
    • Operational benefit: The collector could recover faster from agent/PDU limits that do not present as timeouts.
    • Estimated effort: Medium - targeted transport logic and tests, with device-specific validation needed before enabling by default.
    • Remaining work: Model retry synchronization, choose a deterministic or bounded-random delay policy, and confirm it does not harm reproducibility or timeboxes.
    • Operational benefit: Large fleets would be less likely to create retry bursts against shared network paths or management infrastructure.
    • Estimated effort: Small to medium implementation, but meaningful load testing is required to justify and tune it.

Identity and claim analysis

    • Remaining work: Measure value frequency in a bounded scope, define safe weighting categories, and prevent frequency from overriding hard conflicts or typed strong evidence.
    • Operational benefit: Repeated vendor defaults and low-entropy placeholders would be less likely to cause accidental identity merges.
    • Estimated effort: Large - requires statistics, resolver-policy design, explainability, and labeled production validation.
    • Remaining work: Create a first-class record for mutually incompatible claims, preserve all provenance, connect it to affected entities, and render actionable reports.
    • Operational benefit: Conflicts would be visible and auditable instead of inferred indirectly from cluster outcomes or logs.
    • Estimated effort: Medium to large - schema, resolver, graph projection, output, and diff changes.
    • Remaining work: Trigger a bounded second resolution pass when late strong identifiers arrive, while recording which prior decisions are reconsidered.
    • Operational benefit: Entities initially formed from weak advertisements could be corrected when authoritative device evidence becomes available later in the run.
    • Estimated effort: Large - dependency-aware resolver work with ordering, split/merge, and replay-idempotence risks.
    • Remaining work: Replace irreversible union behavior with provenance-backed components that can retract a weak edge and deterministically rematerialize resulting clusters.
    • Operational benefit: The system could recover from early ambiguous evidence instead of carrying a false merge through final output.
    • Estimated effort: Very large - core resolver redesign requiring append-only decisions, incremental rematerialization, and extensive truth fixtures.
    • Remaining work: Define entity/relationship types and vendor-neutral evidence rules, then map ENTITY-MIB and protocol observations without fabricating unsupported hierarchy.
    • Operational benefit: Inventory and topology views could distinguish hardware containment, logical tenancy, and endpoint attachment accurately.
    • Estimated effort: Very large and incremental - each role needs schema, collector mappings, resolver rules, output, and vendor fixtures.

Open design questions

  • What evidence is sufficient to promote an observed relationship from directness=unresolved to direct or indirect?
  • How should operator-confirmed topology assertions expire or be revalidated?
  • When several VDCs share mgmt0, should the physical attachment remain one interface group with several logical memberships, or become a dedicated attachment entity?
  • How should tunnels and other multipoint overlays be summarized without implying physical adjacency?
  • When one logical workload appears through several hypervisor uplinks, which view best serves operations: workload movement, current forwarding path, or both?
  • Which ENTITY-MIB absence conditions are authoritative on vendors that expose incomplete or inconsistent tables despite successful SNMP responses?
  • At what scale should graph storage move from embedded discovery JSON to a separate indexed artifact?
  • Which graph explanation details are useful at the terminal, and which belong only in JSON or an audit report?
